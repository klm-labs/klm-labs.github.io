# Fraction Feet Inch Calculator Privacy Policy

**Effective date:** 2026-10-29

**Contact:** [klm.labs.inc@gmail.com](mailto:klm.labs.inc@gmail.com)

Fraction Feet Inch Calculator is made by KLM Labs. On your Home Screen it appears as **Construction Calculator**. It does feet, inch and fraction math and construction calculations on your iPhone or iPad. This policy explains what stays on your device, what leaves it, who receives it and why.

## The short version

- **Your calculations are stored on your device.** Numbers you type, answers, your history tape, tool inputs and the Home Screen widget's last answer are stored on your device. No analytics event contains them, but from version 1.0.2 the screen recordings below show the calculator's numbers, its answers and your history.
- **There is no account and no sign-up.** The app never asks for your name or email address. Your usage and purchase records are kept together by random identifiers, so they are linked to you (see below).
- **Usage analytics with a random identifier.** The App Store version sends usage events, such as "a tool was opened", to PostHog, together with technical details about the app and device, and from version 1.1.0 a report when the app crashes. An approximate location (country and city) is worked out from your internet address, which PostHog does not store. No event ever includes a calculation, input or answer.
- **Screen recordings, from version 1.0.2.** The App Store version also records the app's screens as you use them and where you tap. The recordings show the app's own words and numbers, including the calculator; the boxes you type into in the tools are masked on your device (see below).
- **Purchases are handled by Apple.** We never see your card or bank details. RevenueCat receives your App Store purchase records so the app can unlock and restore your plan.
- **No ads and no tracking.** We do not sell data, show adverts, use an advertising identifier, or track you across other apps and websites.

## What is stored on your device

Everything you enter and calculate is stored on your device: the numbers and expressions you type, answers, memory, the history tape, your precision and other settings, the last inputs of each tool, and the last answer shown by the Home Screen widget. The app and its widget share that last answer through storage on your device. No analytics event or purchase record contains any of it, and we cannot retrieve or delete it. From version 1.0.2, though, the screen recordings described under "A recording of the app's screens" show the calculator's numbers, its answers and your history as they appear on screen. Apart from that, it leaves your device only if you share it: you can share your history or a single line, copy an answer into another app, or share a stair layout PDF. Like other app data, it is included in your device's backups (iCloud Backup or a computer backup) if you have backups turned on; those backups are controlled by Apple and your settings, not by us.

The calculator and tools work offline. Copy and paste use your device's clipboard only when you choose Copy or Paste.

## Product analytics (PostHog)

We use product analytics to learn which features are used and where people get stuck, so we can improve the app. [PostHog, Inc.](https://posthog.com/privacy) processes the analytics for KLM Labs on servers in the United States. Analytics, and from version 1.0.2 screen recordings, are sent only by the App Store version of the app (and TestFlight test builds); there is no setting for them in the app.

### What analytics collects

**Events describing what you did, never what you calculated.** The app sends these events: the app was opened; a calculation was completed; a tool was opened (which tool); a result was sent from a tool to the calculator (which tool); a stair layout PDF was shared; a guide was opened (which guide); the app was opened from the widget (which shortcut); the free-trial reminder page was shown, and whether you allowed notifications; the plans screen was shown (where it was opened from and which set of plans it showed); and the result of a purchase or restore (which plan, Yearly or Lifetime, whether a free trial started, and whether it completed, was cancelled, failed or is waiting for approval). From version 1.1.0 it also sends: how long a welcome page, the free-trial reminder page or the plans screen was open and how you left it (next, back, or the app went to the background); that a value in a tool's box couldn't be used when you left the box (which tool, which box and why, such as empty or out of range; never what you typed); which plan you selected on the plans screen, and that you tapped to buy it (which plan, and from where); how long after installing the app your first calculation came; for the plans screen, also its currency, its version and whether you can get the free trial; for a cancelled purchase, how long Apple's purchase sheet was open; for a failed one, the kind of failure and the App Store's error code; for each purchase or restore, whether it happened in Apple's test environment; and, if the app can't open the storage that keeps your history on your device, which step failed and the system's error code, never what your history holds. The analytics software also sends an event when you tap the same spot quickly several times (which screen, and where on it). **No event ever contains a number, an expression, an input or an answer from a calculation.**

**Technical details** that the analytics software adds to each event: the app's name, version and build; the device maker, model and type; the iOS or iPadOS version; the screen size; your language, region and time zone settings; whether the device is on Wi-Fi or cellular data; whether the app came from TestFlight; the analytics software's name and version; the time of the event; and random event and session identifiers. They also include the app's bundle identifier, the operating system's name, the generic device name iOS reports (such as "iPhone" or "iPad"), and whether the app is running in a simulator or was installed from outside the App Store. From version 1.1.0 each event also carries the App Store country, your device's first language, your text size setting and where the app was installed from (the App Store or TestFlight).

**A random identifier** that PostHog creates on your device. It lets PostHog count events from the same installation. It is not your name, email, phone number, Apple Account or the device's advertising identifier. The app also gives it to RevenueCat, so your usage and purchases can be matched (see below).

**An approximate location.** Like any internet service, PostHog sees the IP address your device connects from. PostHog uses that IP address to estimate a country and city when the events arrive, and is set to discard it instead of storing it with the events. **The app does not ask for location permission and never reads your device's GPS.**

**Purchase events.** When you start a trial, buy, renew, cancel or when a plan expires, RevenueCat sends a record of that event (for example the product, its price, RevenueCat's app user ID and Apple's transaction ID) to PostHog under the same random identifier, so we can see how the plans are used. See "Purchases and subscriptions" below.

**A recording of the app's screens, from version 1.0.2.** So we can see where people tap, what they struggle with and where they give up, the app records its screens while you use it: screenshots of the app's own screens, taken as they change, and where you tap. A recording can include any screen of the app. We checked what one shows on a real recording of version 1.0.2: the welcome pages, the plans screen, a purchase, the calculator, the Stairs and Concrete tools, and History. On iPad we checked a recording of version 1.1.0: the calculator with History beside it, a tool and Settings.

| In a recording | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Readable | The app's own words and numbers: titles, buttons, labels, plan names and prices, the calculator's display (what you entered and the answer), the results in the tools, your history, a fraction you pick from a tool's menu, and a tip that pops up over a tool |
| Masked | Every box you type into in the tools: a solid block covers it, so the numbers you type there don't show (the results worked out from them do). |
| Your taps | Where you tap. On the calculator's keypad, the taps show which keys you pressed |
| Not in a recording | The keyboard, which is not part of the app's screen; iOS's own requests, such as the one asking to allow notifications, did not appear in our recordings either |

The app shows no photos; its logo and symbols are drawn shapes and stay visible.

A recording carries the same random identifier and session identifier as your analytics events, so it is linked to you in the same way. Console output and network activity are not recorded. We treat any change to what a recording shows or masks as a change to this policy.

**Crash reports, from version 1.1.0.** If the app crashes, it saves a report on your device and sends it to PostHog the next time you open the app. The report says what went wrong and where in the app's code it happened (for some crashes, the error message), with the same technical details and identifiers as your analytics events, so it is linked to you in the same way. The app puts none of your calculations, inputs or answers into it. We use crash reports only to find and fix crashes, not for usage analytics.

### What analytics never collects

We do not collect your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, files or precise location. No analytics event contains your calculations, inputs, answers, history or anything you type; screen recordings show the calculator and your history, and mask what you type into the tools' boxes, as described above. The app puts none of your calculations, inputs, answers or history into crash reports either.

## Purchases and subscriptions (Apple and RevenueCat)

The app is paid: it offers a yearly subscription with a free trial for eligible new subscribers, and a one-time Lifetime purchase. Payments are made through Apple's App Store with your Apple Account. **Apple processes the payment; KLM Labs never receives or stores your card or bank details.**

[RevenueCat, Inc.](https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy) processes purchase records for KLM Labs so the app can check whether you have access, unlock it and restore it on another device. RevenueCat receives your App Store purchase and subscription records (the product, Apple's transaction IDs, dates, trial and renewal status), a random app user ID that RevenueCat creates (there is no login), which set of plans the app showed you, technical details such as the app version, iOS or iPadOS version and App Store country, and the random PostHog identifier described above so purchase events can be matched with usage analytics. RevenueCat also receives your IP address when the app connects to it, and your device's vendor ID (IDFV) when iOS provides one. iOS gives each app developer one vendor ID per device, the same in all of that developer's apps; it is not the advertising identifier.

We use purchase records only to provide and restore your plan, to understand how the plans are used, and to handle your requests. We do not use them for advertising.

## Reminders and notifications

If you start a free trial, the app can remind you 2 days before it ends. The reminder is a notification scheduled on your device; it is not sent from a server, and no push token is sent to us. Before the plans screen, if the Yearly plan is preselected (from version 1.1.0 only when the Lifetime plan can't be loaded), you can get its free trial and you haven't answered before, the app shows a page about the trial and asks for permission to send notifications. You can say no, or turn notifications off later in Settings; the app works the same either way.

## Sharing a stair layout PDF

The Stairs tool can make a one-page PDF of a stair layout. The PDF is created on your device and opens the system share sheet. It leaves your device only if you choose a destination, which then handles it under its own privacy practices.

## When you email us

If you email us for support or a privacy request, we receive your email address, your name if your email includes it, your message and any attachments you choose to send, such as screenshots. Google processes this mail for us through Gmail. We use it only to answer and handle your request.

## Service providers

PostHog, RevenueCat and, for email, Google (Gmail) process data for KLM Labs. We require the service providers that receive personal data from us to protect it at the same or an equivalent level as this policy describes and Apple's App Review Guidelines require. We do not sell personal data or share it for advertising.

## How long data is kept

On your device, History keeps the newest 1,000 calculations and removes older ones automatically. **History → More (…) → Clear All** removes the history and the widget's last answer; it does not clear the calculator's memory, saved tool inputs or settings. Deleting the app removes everything it stored on the device; copies in your backups, and anything you shared, are managed separately.

We keep analytics events, crash reports, screen recordings, purchase records and support emails only as long as we need them to run, support and improve the app, or as the law requires (for example, purchase records for tax and accounting), and then delete them. Deleting the app does not delete records already sent to PostHog or RevenueCat; to ask us to delete them, see "Your choices and rights" below.

## Children

Fraction Feet Inch Calculator is a tool for building and trade work and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has used the app, contact us and we will delete any records we can find.

## Your choices and rights

- **Delete your history** with History → More (…) → Clear All, or delete the app to remove everything it stored. Device backups, if you have them on, are controlled by Apple and your settings, not by us.
- **Turn off reminders** in your device's Settings → Notifications.
- **Cancel a subscription** in your Apple Account settings; see the [terms of use](/construction-calc/terms/). Deleting the app does not cancel it.
- **Ask us to delete analytics records (events, crash reports and recordings) or purchase records.** Email [klm.labs.inc@gmail.com](mailto:klm.labs.inc@gmail.com). Because these records use random identifiers rather than an account, tell us roughly when you used the app and, for purchases, when you bought, so we can find them. We will tell you what we could find and delete. Apple keeps its own purchase records.
- **Ask what we hold** by emailing the same address.

Depending on where you live, you may have further rights over personal data, such as access, correction, deletion and objection. Contact us and we will respond as the law requires.
